## H01 - VRQ-028 on 2026-10-15

**Question:** It is 15 October 2026. I live in a rooming house and the two washing machines are coin-operated. Are they allowed to charge us to use them?

**Answer:**

> **Short answer:** The documents don't say whether a private rooming house operator can charge for coin-operated washing machines. They don't mention coin-operated machines at all. The only express power to charge for laundry services applies to Homes Victoria or a registered housing agency, not to other operators.
> 
> **Which law applies:** The most recent Act in the search results is the *Residential Tenancies Act 1997* file labelled "rta-1997-v115-2026-10-13.pdf," dated 13 October 2026 [1: 97-109aa115-authorised.pdf]. That is the version in force on 15 October 2026. The text itself does not print an Authorised Version number, so I can only refer to the "v115" in the file name.
> 
> **What the Act says:**
> 
> - **Utility charges (s 108):** An operator may charge for electricity, gas and water used in the room only if the operator pays for them, the room is separately metered, and the charge is no more than the supplier's charge. This section does not apply to shared rooms [1: 97-109aa115-authorised.pdf]. It does not cover washing machines.
> - **Service schedules and itemised accounts (s 109):** If an operator charges for services, it must give a separate schedule of those charges before the resident moves in, and an itemised account of the resident's individual use if the resident uses them [1: 97-109aa115-authorised.pdf].
> - **Service charges for laundry (s 109A):** Homes Victoria or a registered housing agency may impose a service charge for laundry or utility services in a declared rooming house, but only where it is not possible or practicable to measure use accurately. The charge must not exceed the cost of providing the service [1: 97-109aa115-authorised.pdf].
> - **Rent reduction (s 106):** If a rooming house stops providing services, the operator must reduce the rent by the agreed amount or by an amount set by the Tribunal [3: 97-109aa110-authorised.pdf].
> 
> **What the standards say:** From 26 February 2024, a rooming house must provide a washing machine in good working order for every 12 or fewer residents [2: 23-10sra002-authorised.pdf]. This is a standard for the facility, not a rule about whether it can be charged for. Note that this is from the 2023 Regulations (version dated 1 December 2024), not the Act.
> 
> **What you can do or ask:**
> 
> 1. **Ask for the schedule of services.** If the operator charges for the machines, you should have received a separate schedule before you moved in [1: 97-109aa115-authorised.pdf]. Ask for it in writing.
> 2. **Ask for an itemised account** of your individual use if you are charged [1: 97-109aa115-authorised.pdf].
> 3. **Check whether your house is a Homes Victoria or registered agency property.** Only those providers can impose a laundry service charge under s 109A [1: 97-109aa115-authorised.pdf]. If yours is, a charge must not exceed the cost of providing the service, and you can apply to the Tribunal to withdraw or vary it [1: 97-109aa115-authorised.pdf].
> 4. **Report a broken machine.** Urgent repairs include failure or breakdown of an essential service or appliance provided for laundering that the operator supplies. The operator must carry out urgent repairs after being notified [4: 97-109aa111-authorised.pdf].
> 
> **What the documents don't cover:** Whether a private operator may charge coin-based fees for the machines, and how much, is not addressed in the documents I found. If you need a definite answer, check the full Act or ask Consumer Affairs Victoria.

## H02 - NEWSQ-005 on 2026-10-15

**Question:** It is 15 October 2026. I followed up repairs to leaking fixtures and got a notice to vacate the same day. I am renting month to month. What should I check if I think the notice is retaliation for asking for repairs?

**Answer:**

> Based on the *Residential Tenancies Regulations 2021* (Authorised Version v009 as of 30 June 2026), which is the law in force on 15 October 2026, you may challenge a notice to vacate at VCAT if you believe it was given because you tried to exercise your rights as a renter (such as requesting repairs) or due to unlawful discrimination [2: 21-3sra009-authorised.pdf]. 
> 
> Here is exactly what you should check on your Notice to Vacate (Form 6):
> 
> 1. **The Stated Reason and Details (Clause 11)**: Check if the rental provider has inserted a specific legal reason, the relevant section number of the *Residential Tenancies Act 1997*, and a clear explanation of why the notice was given [2: 21-3sra009-authorised.pdf]. The regulations require specific details; it is not enough to just quote general reasons. VCAT may find the notice invalid if it lacks sufficient detail [2: 21-3sra009-authorised.pdf].
> 2. **Documentary Evidence**: Check if any required documentary evidence (approved by the Director of Consumer Affairs Victoria) is attached to the notice, as some grounds require it [2: 21-3sra009-authorised.pdf].
> 3. **The Termination Date and Notice Period (Clause 10)**: Verify that the termination date allows for the minimum number of days' notice required under the Act, taking into account how the notice was delivered and when you are expected to receive it [2: 21-3sra009-authorised.pdf].
> 4. **Grounds for Challenge**: Confirm whether your suspicion aligns with the valid grounds listed on the form—specifically, that you believe the notice was given because you tried to exercise your rights as a renter or due to unlawful discrimination [2: 21-3sra009-authorised.pdf].
> 
> If you find discrepancies or believe the notice is retaliatory, you should seek advice immediately from a community legal organisation or contact Consumer Affairs Victoria at 1300 558 181 [2: 21-3sra009-authorised.pdf].

<details><summary>Gold answer</summary>

A landlord can't end a lease without a reason. The notice to vacate must be on the official form, state a reason allowed by the Act, and, for some reasons (such as sale, major repairs, demolition, or the owner or their family moving in), come with supporting evidence. The Act has no general rule that a retaliatory notice is void; its express rules cover only a few notice types (threats and intimidation, and eligibility notices in NRAS or affordable-housing tenancies). The renter should check the stated reason, the evidence and the dates, and keep records of the repair requests. If the notice was given for one of the evidence-backed reasons (sale, repairs, demolition, business use, the owner moving in, or public purposes), they can challenge it at VCAT within 30 days after it is given; for other reasons, they can challenge it when the landlord applies for a possession order. If the landlord applies to evict, VCAT must be satisfied the landlord was entitled to give the notice and that eviction is reasonable and proportionate, and it considers the landlord's or agent's behaviour, so the renter can raise retaliation there.

</details>

For insurance, the owner would need to check their policy or ask their insurer.

## H15 - VRQ-003 on 2025-10-01

**Question:** It is 1 October 2025. Our landlord gave us a notice to vacate because they were selling. The buyer pulled out, but the landlord hasn't withdrawn the notice. Do we still have to leave?

**Answer:**

> ## What the law says
> 
> As at **1 October 2025**, the applicable document is **Residential Tenancies Act 1997, Authorised Version 110 (6 August 2025)**. [1: 97-109aa110-authorised.pdf]
> 
> A notice to vacate because the premises are to be sold is permitted only where, immediately after the termination date, the premises are to be **sold or offered for sale with vacant possession**. [1: 97-109aa110-authorised.pdf] The notice must generally give at least **60 days’ notice** and include supporting documentary evidence. [1: 97-109aa110-authorised.pdf]
> 
> The landlord’s notice is **not automatically withdrawn** just because the buyer pulled out. Under section 91ZZQ, it is withdrawn only if the landlord gives you a written withdrawal notice, signed by the landlord and by you. [1: 97-109aa110-authorised.pdf]
> 
> Therefore, based only on the documents, the notice remains on foot unless it is formally withdrawn or successfully challenged. However, the documents do not say that you must leave automatically merely because the landlord has not withdrawn it: if you do not leave, the landlord may apply to VCAT for a possession order, and VCAT decides whether the notice is valid and whether you must leave. [1: 97-109aa110-authorised.pdf]
> 
> ## What you can do or ask
> 
> - **Ask the landlord in writing to withdraw the notice**, using a written withdrawal signed by both the landlord and you. [1: 97-109aa110-authorised.pdf]
> - If the property is no longer genuinely going to be sold or offered for sale with vacant possession, you may have grounds to challenge whether the notice was valid under section 91ZZB. The documents do not decide whether a failed sale, by itself, invalidates an already-issued notice. [1: 97-109aa110-authorised.pdf]
> - You may apply to VCAT challenging the notice within **30 days after it was given**, and VCAT can determine whether it is valid. [1: 97-109aa110-authorised.pdf]
> - Do not assume the landlord can physically remove you: if you do not leave, the landlord must obtain a possession order, and only Victoria Police can carry out a forcible eviction under a warrant for possession. [1: 97-109aa110-authorised.pdf]

<details><summary>Gold answer</summary>

A landlord can give a notice to vacate because the home will be sold or offered for sale with vacant possession, or within 14 days of signing a sale contract (or of its conditions being met). The notice must give at least 60 days, be in the official form, state the reason, come with supporting evidence, and give the end date. On a fixed-term lease without a break clause, it has no effect if the end date is before the lease ends. The buyer pulling out does not cancel the notice by itself: a notice is withdrawn only by a written withdrawal signed by both the landlord and the renter. Whether the notice still holds depends on its stated ground and evidence: if the landlord still plans to sell with vacant possession it may stand, but if it relied on the failed contract the evidence may no longer support it. The renters can ask the landlord to withdraw it in writing, signed by both of them, or challenge it at VCAT within 30 days after it is given.

</details>
