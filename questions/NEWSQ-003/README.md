# NEWSQ-003

**Role:** partly outside the law documents  
**Set:** held-out (chosen, and its gold written, after the fix was frozen)

## Situation

A Melbourne renter received seven days notice requiring them to be home for a weekday two-and-a-half-hour smoke-alarm inspection window, with a threatened $90 re-inspection fee. The agent gave the notice in writing seven days ahead. The agent holds a key.

## Question

My agent says I must be home for a smoke alarm check on a weekday between 10 am and 12:30 pm, or pay a $90 fee to rebook. Do I have to be there myself to let the inspector in, and can they charge me the $90?

## Gold — scenario date 2025-12-03

Status: approved 5 November 2026. Must mention revised 5 November 2026 (a point no model states with the passages in hand moved to optional credit).

**Question as asked:** Today is Wednesday 3 December 2025. My agent says I must be home for a smoke alarm check on a weekday between 10 am and 12:30 pm, or pay a $90 fee to rebook. Do I have to be there myself to let the inspector in, and can they charge me the $90?

**Must cite (version in force on this date):**
- Residential Tenancies Act 1997, Authorised Version No. 111
- Residential Tenancies Regulations 2021, Authorised Version No. 005

**Correct answer (short):** You don't have to be home yourself. Your duty is to let the landlord or agent in when they enter properly, not to attend. Checking the smoke alarms is the landlord's own duty (s 68AA, in force since 25 November 2025, and the smoke-alarm term in the standard agreement), and entry to carry out a duty under the Act is allowed between 8 am and 6 pm on any day except a public holiday with at least 24 hours' written notice that says why they're coming. Seven days' notice in writing meets that, so the agent, who has a key, can bring the inspector in at the notified time whether or not you are there. Nothing in the Act lets the agent charge you a $90 rebooking fee; the testing is the landlord's cost. If you actually refused a lawful entry, the landlord's route would be a breach-of-duty claim and compensation through VCAT, not a fee of their choosing. What you can do: reply in writing that you don't object to entry at the notified time and they may use their key, and that you don't accept the $90 charge. If the window is a real problem, you can propose another time, or apply to VCAT for an order limiting entry days and times.

**Must mention:**
- The renter does not have to be present; the duty is to permit entry, and the agent can enter with the inspector on proper notice (saying the agent can use the key, or that the renter need only not obstruct entry, counts).
- Testing or checking smoke alarms is the landlord's duty, and entry to carry out a duty needs at least 24 hours' written notice, within 8 am to 6 pm, which the seven days given satisfies.

**Optional (credit, not required):**
- Nothing in the Act provides for a $90 re-inspection fee; the landlord's only route to any cost is a breach-of-duty or compensation claim through the Tribunal if entry was refused.

**Must not say:**
- That the renter must personally be home to let the inspector in.
- That the $90 fee is a charge the renter has to pay.
- A wrong version of the Act or Regulations.

**Pass / fail rule:** Pass if the answer covers everything under "must mention", names the document and version it relies on (from the "must cite" list), and says nothing on the "must not say" list. Otherwise fail.
