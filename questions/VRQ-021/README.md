# VRQ-021

**Role:** date-sensitive  
**Set:** main (one of the 10 questions the study was developed on)

## Situation

A renter says most household sockets share a circuit and using ordinary appliances together trips the circuit breaker. The lease was signed after 29 March 2021 on the standard form, so it contains the electrical safety check term.

## Question

Most of the power points in my rental are on one circuit, and the breaker trips when we run the kettle and the heater together. Is there a rule about how many circuits a rental needs, and what can I ask for?

**Boundary:** 29 Mar 2023: Sch 4 cl 9 electrical standard applies

## Facts the question leaves open

- The standard does not prescribe a number of circuits; whether frequent tripping indicates a safety fault needs an electrician's inspection.
- Whether the outlets and lighting circuits are connected to a compliant circuit breaker and RCD.
- Date of the problem relative to 29 March 2023.
- Whether the agreement contains the standard electrical safety term, and the date of the last check.
- Whether the tripping is a dangerous electrical fault (urgent repair) or a non-urgent repair.

## Gold — scenario date 2023-03-01

Status: approved 24 October 2026.

**Must cite (version in force on this date):**
- Residential Tenancies Act 1997, Authorised Version No. 101 (urgent repairs, s 3(1); safety-related duties, s 68A(1))
- Residential Tenancies Regulations 2021, Authorised Version No. 002 (standard form term 14, electrical safety checks)

**Correct answer (short):** The rental rules don't set a minimum number of circuits. The renter can ask the landlord to repair the problem, and if it's a dangerous electrical fault it counts as an urgent repair. They can also ask for the date of the last electrical safety check, which under the standard agreement terms must be done every 2 years; if none was done in the last 2 years, the landlord must arrange one.

**Must mention:**
- No rule on the number of circuits.
- Next step: request a repair (urgent if the fault is dangerous) and/or ask for the date of the last electrical safety check.

**Must not say:**
- Any minimum number of circuits, or that sharing a circuit is in itself illegal.
- A wrong version of the Act or Regulations.
- That the circuit breaker and safety switch (RCD) standard already applies on this date. (Mentioning that it starts on 29 March 2023 is not required.)

**Pass / fail rule:** Pass if the answer says there is no circuit-count rule, gives at least one of the renter's next steps, names the document and version it relies on (from the "must cite" list), and says nothing on the "must not say" list. Otherwise fail.

## Gold — scenario date 2026-10-15

Status: approved 24 October 2026.

**Must cite (version in force on this date):**
- Residential Tenancies Regulations 2021, Authorised Version No. 009 (Sch 4 cl 9, electrical safety standard)
- Residential Tenancies Act 1997, Authorised Version No. 115 (urgent repairs, s 3(1); electrical safety check, s 68A(1A))

**Correct answer (short):** The rental rules don't set a minimum number of circuits. Since 29 March 2023, all power points and lighting circuits must be connected to a switchboard circuit breaker and a safety switch (RCD), so the renter can ask whether the home meets that standard. The renter can ask the landlord to repair the problem, and if it's a dangerous electrical fault it counts as an urgent repair. They can also ask for the date of the last electrical safety check, which the landlord must give in writing and which must be done every 2 years; if none was done in the last 2 years, the landlord must arrange one.

**Must mention:**
- No rule on the number of circuits.
- The circuit breaker and safety switch (RCD) standard applies.
- Next step: request a repair (urgent if the fault is dangerous) and/or ask for the date of the last electrical safety check.

**Must not say:**
- Any minimum number of circuits, or that sharing a circuit is in itself illegal.
- A wrong version of the Act or Regulations.

**Pass / fail rule:** Pass if the answer says there is no circuit-count rule, mentions the circuit breaker and safety switch standard, gives at least one of the renter's next steps, names the document and version it relies on (from the "must cite" list), and says nothing on the "must not say" list. Otherwise fail.
