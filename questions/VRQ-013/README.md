# VRQ-013

**Role:** law unchanged across versions  
**Set:** held-out (chosen, and its gold written, after the fix was frozen)

## Situation

After a tenancy with pets, an agent demands a professional-cleaning receipt under a pet clause even though the renter says the carpet was restored to its starting condition. The lease is on the standard form and started in 2024.

## Question

My lease has a pet clause saying I must have the carpets professionally cleaned when I leave. I cleaned them myself and they're back to how they were when I moved in, but the agent wants a receipt from a professional cleaner. Do I have to pay for one, and what would help show the carpets' condition?

**Question as asked:** My lease has a pet clause saying I must have the carpets professionally cleaned when I leave. I cleaned them myself and they're back to how they were when I moved in, but the agent wants a receipt from a professional cleaner. Do I have to pay for one, and what would help show the carpets' condition?

**Must cite (version in force on this date):**
- Residential Tenancies Act 1997, Authorised Version No. 115
- Residential Tenancies Regulations 2021, Authorised Version No. 009

**Correct answer (short):** A clause that makes you arrange professional cleaning is only enforceable in two situations: the carpets were professionally cleaned just before you moved in and you were told so, or professional cleaning is actually needed to get them back to the condition they were in at the start, judged against the condition report and allowing for fair wear and tear (reg 12, under s 27C). The standard agreement bars the landlord from requiring professional cleaning outside those cases, and the fact that you had a pet doesn't change the test. Your legal duty is to leave the place reasonably clean and in the same condition as when you moved in, fair wear and tear excepted (s 63). So if the carpets are back to their starting condition, you don't owe a receipt. What decides it is evidence of the starting condition: the entry condition report, your own move-in photos, and whether the agent told you in writing that the carpets had been professionally cleaned before you moved in. Reply to the agent with that evidence and your exit photos; if they claim from the bond, they must give you bond claim evidence, and you can dispute it at VCAT.

**Must mention:**
- Professional cleaning can only be required if it was done before the tenancy and the renter was told, or if it is needed to restore the pre-tenancy condition allowing for fair wear and tear (reg 12).
- The renter's duty is to leave the premises reasonably clean and in the same condition as at the start, fair wear and tear excepted (s 63).
- The entry condition report (or move-in photos) is the evidence that decides the starting condition.

**Must not say:**
- That the renter must pay for professional cleaning simply because the clause says so or because they had a pet.
- A wrong version of the Act or Regulations.
