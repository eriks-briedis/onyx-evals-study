# VRQ-001

**Role:** needs more facts  
**Set:** main (one of the 10 questions the study was developed on)

## Situation

A renter submits a pet request for an apartment. The agent refuses because the owner is allergic to dogs; the renter expects the owner would visit infrequently. The renter used the official pet request form. It is three weeks since the agent got the request, and nothing has been filed at VCAT.

## Question

I sent the official request form to keep a dog in my apartment three weeks ago. The agent said no because the owner is allergic, but the owner hardly ever visits. Can they refuse?

## Gold — scenario date 2026-10-15

Status: approved 26 October 2026.

**Must cite (version in force on this date):**
- Residential Tenancies Act 1997, Authorised Version No. 115 (ss 71B–71E)

**Correct answer (short):** A renter asks to keep a pet using the official request form, and the landlord must not unreasonably refuse. The agent saying no is not enough to refuse: the landlord is taken to have consented unless they apply to VCAT within 14 days after getting the request. Only VCAT can decide that a refusal is reasonable, looking at things like the type of pet and the property; the owner's allergy doesn't decide it automatically. Three weeks have passed, so unless the landlord applied to VCAT within the first 14 days, consent is taken as given. VCAT also considers whether refusing is allowed under any other law, and because it's an apartment, the owners corporation rules may also cover pets; they are separate from the rental law. The renter can ask the agent in writing whether the landlord applied to VCAT within 14 days, and if not, say that consent is taken as given, and check the owners corporation rules before bringing the dog.

**Must mention:**
- The landlord is taken to have consented unless they apply to VCAT within 14 days of getting the request.
- With three weeks passed, consent is taken as given unless the landlord applied to VCAT within the 14 days.
- At least one next step: ask the agent in writing whether a VCAT application was made, or check the owners corporation rules.

**Optional (credit, not required):**
- Owners corporation rules for the apartment may also apply to pets (VCAT considers whether refusal is allowed under any other law).

**Must not say:**
- That the agent's refusal stands or is final.
- That the renter must apply to VCAT.
- That the owner's allergy automatically decides it (either way).
- A wrong version of the Act.
