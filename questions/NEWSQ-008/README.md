# NEWSQ-008

**Role:** story  
**Set:** main (one of the 10 questions the study was developed on)

## Situation

A Victorian applicant applied at the advertised weekly rent. Three days later the agent incorrectly said the application had been withdrawn, and the applicant found the property advertised again at a higher price. The applicant hears that another applicant offered more than the advertised rent. The email and relisting happen just before the scenario date.

## Question

I applied for a rental at the advertised price, then got an email saying I had withdrawn even though I had not. The property is now listed at a higher rent, and I heard someone else offered more than the advertised price. Can I challenge what happened, and what should I save as evidence?

**Boundary:** 25 Nov 2025: s 30F(4) (accepting higher unsolicited offers) commences

## Facts the question leaves open

- Australian Consumer Law (Victoria) substantive misleading-conduct provisions and complaint mechanism not fetched.
- False withdrawal/relisting is an allegation, not proof; s 30F/30G passages alone do not resolve it.

## Gold — scenario date 2025-10-01

Status: approved 26 October 2026. Must mention revised 30 October 2026 (points needing sources outside the library made optional or reworded).

**Must cite (version in force on this date):**
- Residential Tenancies Act 1997, Authorised Version No. 110 (ss 30F, 30G)

**Correct answer (short):** Rent must be advertised as a fixed amount, but relisting at a new, higher fixed amount isn't illegal by itself. The landlord or agent must not ask for or invite offers above the advertised rent. If someone else offered more without being asked, the rental Act did not ban the agent from accepting it on this date; it would only be banned if the agent had invited it. The rental Act has no specific rule about falsely marking an application as withdrawn. Its ban on misleading claims about rent covers only landlords and agents not acting in trade or commerce; a professional agent is covered instead by the Australian Consumer Law ban on misleading conduct, which isn't in these documents. There's no right to be given the property: challenging means complaining, not forcing the agent to rent it to them. The applicant can save the original ad (price and date), the application confirmation, the "withdrawn" email and the new listing, ask the agent in writing to explain, and complain to Consumer Affairs Victoria.

**Must mention:**
- Relisting at a higher fixed rent isn't illegal by itself.
- Asking for or inviting offers above the advertised rent is banned.
- If the higher offer was unsolicited, the rental Act did not ban accepting it on this date (only inviting it was banned).
- The false "withdrawn" email isn't covered by a specific rental-law rule.

**Optional (credit, not required):**
- The false "withdrawn" email may be misleading conduct (Australian Consumer Law for a professional agent).
- Saving the evidence: the original ad, the application confirmation, the "withdrawn" email and the new listing.
- Complaining to Consumer Affairs Victoria.

**Must not say:**
- That relisting at a higher rent is illegal by itself.
- That accepting a higher unsolicited offer is illegal on this date.
- That the applicant can force the agent to rent the property to them.
- A wrong version of the Act.
