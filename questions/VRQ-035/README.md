# VRQ-035

**Role:** date-sensitive  
**Set:** main (one of the 10 questions the study was developed on)

## Situation

A poster reports apparent mould in a Victorian rental advertisement's photographs.

## Question

The photos in a rental ad look like they show mould in one of the bedrooms. Are they allowed to advertise it like that, and is the photo enough to prove the place doesn't meet the minimum standards?

**Boundary:** 25 Nov 2025: s 65B (advertising non-compliant premises) commences

## Facts the question leaves open

- Date the advertisement was published, relative to 25 November 2025.
- Whether the visible growth is mould and whether it is caused by or related to the building structure.
- What the provider or agent believed about compliance when advertising.
- Whether the person has signed an agreement or moved in.

## Gold — scenario date 2025-10-01

Status: approved 26 October 2026. Must mention revised 30 October 2026 (points needing sources outside the library made optional or reworded).

**Must cite (version in force on this date):**
- Residential Tenancies Act 1997, Authorised Version No. 110 (s 65A, s 91L)
- Residential Tenancies Regulations 2021, Authorised Version No. 004 (Sch 4 cl 8, mould and dampness)

**Correct answer (short):** Rented homes must meet the rental minimum standards, which include every room being free from mould and damp caused by or related to the building structure. A photo alone doesn't show whether it is mould or whether the building causes it. The landlord must make sure the home meets the minimum standards by the day the renter moves in. If it doesn't, the renter can request urgent repairs, and a renter who hasn't yet moved in can end the agreement because the home doesn't meet the minimum standards. Before signing, the renter can ask the agent about the mould and check it at the inspection.

**Must mention:**
- The mould and damp minimum standard (caused by or related to the building structure).
- The photo alone doesn't prove a breach (saying the documents don't make a photo proof and it needs checking in person also counts).
- At least one next step: ask the agent / check at the inspection, request urgent repairs after moving in, or end the agreement before moving in.

**Must not say:**
- That the photo alone proves the ad or the property is illegal.
- That the landlord or agent is banned from advertising a property that doesn't meet the minimum standards (that rule doesn't apply on this date). (Mentioning that it starts on 25 November 2025 is not required.)
- A wrong version of the Act or Regulations.

**Pass / fail rule:** Pass if the answer covers everything under "must mention", names the document and version it relies on (from the "must cite" list), and says nothing on the "must not say" list. Otherwise fail.

## Gold — scenario date 2026-02-01

Status: approved 26 October 2026. Must mention revised 30 October 2026 (points needing sources outside the library made optional or reworded).

**Must cite (version in force on this date):**
- Residential Tenancies Act 1997, Authorised Version No. 111 (s 65B, s 65A, s 91L)
- Residential Tenancies Regulations 2021, Authorised Version No. 005 (Sch 4 cl 8, mould and dampness)

**Correct answer (short):** The landlord or agent must not advertise a home for rent unless, when advertising, they reasonably believe it meets the rental minimum standards. Those standards include every room being free from mould and damp caused by or related to the building structure. A photo alone doesn't show whether it is mould, whether the building causes it, or what the landlord believed. The landlord must also make sure the home meets the minimum standards by the day the renter moves in. If it doesn't, the renter can request urgent repairs, and a renter who hasn't yet moved in can end the agreement because the home doesn't meet the minimum standards. Before signing, the renter can ask the agent about the mould and check it at the inspection.

**Must mention:**
- The advertising rule: the landlord or agent must reasonably believe the home meets the minimum standards when advertising.
- The mould and damp minimum standard (caused by or related to the building structure).
- The photo alone doesn't prove a breach (saying the documents don't make a photo proof and it needs checking in person also counts).
- At least one next step: ask the agent / check at the inspection, request urgent repairs after moving in, or end the agreement before moving in.

**Must not say:**
- That the photo alone proves the ad or the property is illegal.
- That the advertising rule requires an inspection certificate.
- A wrong version of the Act or Regulations.

**Pass / fail rule:** Pass if the answer covers everything under "must mention", names the document and version it relies on (from the "must cite" list), and says nothing on the "must not say" list. Otherwise fail.
