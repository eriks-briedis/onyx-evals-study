# VRQ-016

**Role:** law unchanged across versions  
**Set:** held-out (chosen, and its gold written, after the fix was frozen)

## Situation

A renter agrees to a cleaning amount being deducted from the bond. The bond is returned in full, and the agent later sends a separate invoice. The invoice arrived a month after the bond was repaid. The renter did not sign a bond claim form for the cleaning amount.

## Question

When I moved out I agreed the agent could take an amount for cleaning out of my bond, but I never signed a bond claim form for it. Then my full bond came back to me, and a month later the agent sent a separate invoice for the cleaning. Do I have to pay it? Is agreeing to a bond deduction the same as agreeing to pay an invoice?

**Question as asked:** When I moved out I agreed the agent could take an amount for cleaning out of my bond, but I never signed a bond claim form for it. Then my full bond came back to me, and a month later the agent sent a separate invoice for the cleaning. Do I have to pay it? Is agreeing to a bond deduction the same as agreeing to pay an invoice?

**Must cite (version in force on this date):**
- Residential Tenancies Act 1997, Authorised Version No. 115

**Correct answer (short):** No, they're not the same thing. Agreeing to a bond deduction means agreeing to a claim on the bond: the landlord or agent gives you bond claim evidence, and a claim to the Bond Authority that pays money to the landlord is made jointly or on a form you sign that states the amount (s 411). Your bond came back in full, so either no claim for the cleaning was made or the Authority repaid it without a dispute (s 411AC). That doesn't wipe the slate: the Act says recovering from a bond doesn't stop a landlord claiming more as compensation, and the same logic applies when nothing was taken (s 214). But an invoice is not a debt you must pay just because it arrives. If you don't agree, the landlord's route is to claim compensation through VCAT, where they must show you didn't leave the place reasonably clean, judged against its condition at the start of the tenancy (s 63; the cleaning claim in s 411AB(c)). What to do: reply in writing that you don't accept the invoice, say what you understood the bond arrangement to be, keep your exit photos and the condition report, and let them take it to VCAT if they want to pursue it.

**Must mention:**
- Agreeing to a bond deduction is agreement to a claim on the bond (a claim form stating the amount, or bond claim evidence), not agreement to pay a separate invoice.
- The test at VCAT is whether the premises were left reasonably clean, judged against their condition at the start (s 63, s 411AB(c)).

**Optional (credit, not required):**
- Full repayment of the bond does not by itself bar the landlord from seeking compensation (s 214), but the invoice is not enforceable on its own: a disputed claim has to go to VCAT.

**Must not say:**
- That the renter must pay the invoice because they agreed to a bond deduction.
- That the full bond repayment means the landlord can never claim anything more.
- A wrong version of the Act.
