# WPQ-005

**Role:** needs more facts  
**Set:** main (one of the 10 questions the study was developed on)

## Situation

The named tenant has taken in a housemate without telling the agent and now wants the housemate to leave.

## Question

I let a friend move into my rental without telling the agent. Now I want them out. What do I have to do?

## Gold — scenario date 2026-10-15

Status: approved 26 October 2026. Must mention revised 30 October 2026 (points needing sources outside the library made optional or reworded). Revised 4 November 2026: the sub-letting-makes-the-renter-the-landlord point and the lock-out point moved to optional. Both rest on a two-step inference from the s 3(1) definitions (the renter is the friend's residential rental provider, so s 91P binds the renter) that retrieval never surfaces from the lay question and that no model states even when handed the passages (findings note, section 14). Must-not-say still fails a fixed notice period, locking the friend out, or saying the friend has no rights.

**Must cite (version in force on this date):**
- Residential Tenancies Act 1997, Authorised Version No. 115 (ss 3(1), 81, 91P, 91R; s 91S if raised)

**Correct answer (short):** It depends on the arrangement, and the law doesn't give one answer without more facts. A rental agreement can be written or unwritten. If the renter let part of the home to the friend (for example, a room that is only theirs, in exchange for rent), that can be a sub-letting: the renter then counts as the friend's landlord under the Act and can only end it through the Act's notice process, and forcing a renter out other than under the Act is an offence. Sub-letting without the landlord's written consent is invalid unless VCAT decides consent wasn't needed, and it may put the renter in breach of their own lease; these documents don't say what an invalid sub-letting means for the friend's position, which is another reason the answer isn't settled. If the friend only shares the home without their own space, the Act may not apply and ordinary agreement rules decide it, which aren't in these documents. What decides it: whether the friend has a space that's only theirs, what was agreed, and what they pay. The renter shouldn't lock the friend out or remove their belongings. They should work out what was agreed and paid, and can get advice (for example, from Tenants Victoria or Consumer Affairs Victoria) before giving any notice.

**Must mention:**
- The answer depends on the arrangement, and at least one deciding fact is named: a space that's only theirs, what was agreed, or what they pay.
- Sub-letting needs the landlord's written consent.

**Optional (credit, not required):**
- If it is a sub-letting, the renter counts as the friend's landlord and must follow the Act's process.
- Don't lock the friend out or remove their belongings.
- Getting advice, for example from Tenants Victoria or Consumer Affairs Victoria.

**Must not say:**
- A fixed notice period or process for ending the friend's occupancy stated as certain (for example, "give 28 days notice").
- That the renter can lock the friend out or remove their belongings.
- That the friend has no rights because they're not on the lease.
- That the friend can simply apply to VCAT to be added to the lease. (Under s 91S an occupant not on the lease can apply only in listed situations, such as a possession application, or the renter leaving, abandoning the home or giving notice to leave.)
- A wrong version of the Act.

**Pass / fail rule:** Pass if the answer covers everything under "must mention", names the document and version it relies on (from the "must cite" list), and says nothing on the "must not say" list. Otherwise fail.
