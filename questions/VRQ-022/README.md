# VRQ-022

**Role:** date-sensitive  
**Set:** held-out (chosen, and its gold written, after the fix was frozen)

## Situation

After signing a lease and collecting keys, a renter finds damp, mould-like growth and rotting timber before moving in. The renter has not moved any belongings in or stayed there yet.

## Question

I signed a lease and picked up the keys, but before moving in I found damp, mould and rotting timber in the house. I haven't moved any of my things in or stayed there yet. What are my options? Can I cancel, ask for repairs, or ask for lower rent?

**Boundary:** 29 Mar 2021: rental minimum standards (s 65A and the Regulations) and s 91L commence. Version 088 prints s 65A only as amending text in its endnotes, not in force.

**Question as asked:** This is happening now, on 15 October 2019. I signed a lease and picked up the keys, but before moving in I found damp, mould and rotting timber in the house. I haven't moved any of my things in or stayed there yet. What are my options? Can I cancel, ask for repairs, or ask for lower rent?

**Must cite (version in force on this date):**
- Residential Tenancies Act 1997, Authorised Version No. 088

**Correct answer (short):** On this date there were no "rental minimum standards" in the Act; the mould and damp standard came in with the 29 March 2021 reforms. What applied was the landlord's duty to keep the premises in good repair, and a tenant who has not yet moved in can end the agreement by giving the landlord a notice of termination on the ground that the premises are not in good repair or are unfit to live in (s 226). So you can cancel, or you can keep the lease and ask for repairs under the good-repair duty; if the damage makes the home unsafe or there's a serious roof leak or flooding, that counts as urgent repairs, which the landlord must fix straight away (and which you can arrange yourself and be reimbursed for, within limits, if the landlord won't). A rent reduction isn't something these documents give you a right to for this; it would need the landlord's agreement or a Tribunal order. Document the damp, mould and rotten timber with photos before deciding, because the cancellation ground depends on the premises really being in disrepair or unfit, not just unclean.

**Must mention:**
- A renter who has not moved in can end the agreement by notice on the ground that the premises are not in good repair or are unfit for habitation (s 226).
- The landlord must keep the premises in good repair (s 68), and the renter can ask for repairs; serious damage that makes the home unsafe can be urgent repairs.
- That whether the renter can cancel turns on the premises being in disrepair or unfit, not merely unclean (naming photos or an inspection as the evidence also counts).

**Must not say:**
- That the home must meet the rental minimum standards, or the mould and damp standard, on this date (those did not exist yet).
- That the renter has no choice but to move in and pay rent.
- A wrong version of the Act.

**Question as asked:** I signed a lease and picked up the keys, but before moving in I found damp, mould and rotting timber in the house. I haven't moved any of my things in or stayed there yet. What are my options? Can I cancel, ask for repairs, or ask for lower rent?

**Must cite (version in force on this date):**
- Residential Tenancies Act 1997, Authorised Version No. 115
- Residential Tenancies Regulations 2021, Authorised Version No. 009

**Correct answer (short):** The landlord must make sure the home meets the rental minimum standards on or before the day you move in. One of those standards is that every room is free from mould and damp caused by or related to the building structure, and rotting timber may also mean the home is not structurally sound or in good repair. Because you have not taken possession, you can end the agreement by giving the landlord a notice of termination on the ground that the premises do not meet the minimum standards, are not in good repair, or are unfit to live in (s 91L). If you'd rather keep the lease, you can move in and request urgent repairs to bring the home up to the standards (s 65A(2)), and the landlord must keep the home in good repair. A rent reduction isn't a right these documents give you for this; it would need the landlord's agreement or a Tribunal order. Whichever way you go, record the damp, mould and timber with photos and raise it with the agent in writing before the move-in date.

**Must mention:**
- The rental minimum standards, including the mould and damp standard (caused by or related to the building structure), must be met by the day the renter moves in (s 65A).
- A renter who has not taken possession can end the agreement by notice because the premises do not meet the minimum standards, are not in good repair, or are unfit for habitation (s 91L).
- The repair route: request urgent repairs for the minimum-standard failures (s 65A(2)) or repairs under the good-repair duty.

**Must not say:**
- That the renter must move in and pay rent before they can do anything.
- That the law gives the renter a right to lower rent for this (saying a reduction would need agreement or a Tribunal order is fine).
- A wrong version of the Act or Regulations.
