# VRQ-020

**Role:** law unchanged across versions  
**Set:** held-out (chosen, and its gold written, after the fix was frozen)

## Situation

A broken tap forces a renter to shut off the property's water. The emergency repair contact refuses to act because the owner only allows preferred tradespeople. The renter has called the emergency contact twice on the same day.

## Question

A tap broke and I had to turn off the water to the whole house. The emergency repair contact says the owner only lets their own tradespeople do repairs, and none of them can come yet. Do I have to wait, or can I get it fixed myself?

**Question as asked:** A tap broke and I had to turn off the water to the whole house. The emergency repair contact says the owner only lets their own tradespeople do repairs, and none of them can come yet. Do I have to wait, or can I get it fixed myself?

**Must cite (version in force on this date):**
- Residential Tenancies Act 1997, Authorised Version No. 115
- Residential Tenancies Regulations 2021, Authorised Version No. 009

**Correct answer (short):** Losing the water supply, or a burst water service, is an urgent repair under the Act. The landlord has to arrange urgent repairs immediately, and a lease term that forces you to use a tradesperson the landlord nominates is a prohibited term, so a "preferred tradies only" policy can't hold up an urgent repair. If you've taken reasonable steps to get the landlord or agent to fix it straight away and they won't or can't, you can arrange the repair yourself (s 72). You then give the landlord written notice of the repair and the cost within 7 days, and the landlord must reimburse the reasonable cost, up to the prescribed cap, within 7 days of your notice. If the landlord won't reimburse you, or you can't afford to pay up front, you can apply to VCAT for an urgent repairs order. What to do now: put your request in writing with the time you called, give the agent one more chance to send someone today, and if nobody comes, get a licensed plumber in and keep the invoice.

**Must mention:**
- No water supply, or a burst water service, is an urgent repair.
- After reasonable steps to get the landlord or agent to act immediately, the renter can arrange the repair and must be reimbursed the reasonable cost (up to the prescribed amount) within 7 days of written notice (s 72).
- A term requiring the renter to use a tradesperson nominated by the landlord is a prohibited term (reg 11(e)), or equivalently, the preferred-tradesperson policy cannot stop an urgent repair.

**Must not say:**
- That the renter must wait for the landlord's preferred tradesperson.
- That the renter cannot recover the cost of a repair they arrange.
- A wrong version of the Act or Regulations.
