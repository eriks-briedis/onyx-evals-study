**Role:** partly outside the law documents

## Situation

A rental is repeatedly burgled during move-out while the lease is still active; plumbing and wiring are damaged, preventing ordinary cleaning. About three weeks are left on the fixed-term lease.

## Question

Our rental was broken into several times while we were moving out, and the lease hasn't ended yet. The burglars damaged the plumbing and wiring, so we can't even clean properly. What are our options before the lease ends?

**Question as asked:** Our rental was broken into several times while we were moving out, and the lease hasn't ended yet. The burglars damaged the plumbing and wiring, so we can't even clean properly. What are our options before the lease ends?

**Must cite (version in force on this date):**
- Residential Tenancies Act 1997, Authorised Version No. 115

**Correct answer (short):** Tell the landlord or agent in writing straight away about the break-ins and the damage; you must report damage and breakdowns as soon as you become aware of them (s 72AA). Damaged plumbing and wiring that cut off water or leave a dangerous electrical fault are urgent repairs: the landlord must arrange them immediately, and if they don't, you can arrange them yourself after reasonable attempts and be reimbursed the reasonable cost within 7 days of giving written notice (s 72). If the damage has left the home unfit to live in or unsafe, you can end the lease early by giving a notice of intention to vacate, and the termination date can be the day you give it (s 91ZD); that applies even though your fixed term hasn't ended. If the home is still habitable, the lease runs to its end date and you remain responsible for rent and for leaving it reasonably clean as far as practicable, but not for damage you didn't cause. Who pays for the burglary damage itself, and whether insurance covers it, isn't settled by these documents.

**Must mention:**
- Report the damage to the landlord or agent in writing as soon as practicable (s 72AA).
- Damaged plumbing and wiring are urgent repairs the landlord must fix immediately; the renter can arrange them and be reimbursed if the landlord does not (s 72).
- If the home is unfit for habitation or unsafe, the renter can end the lease by a notice of intention to vacate with an immediate termination date, fixed term or not (s 91ZD).

**Must not say:**
- That the renter is liable to repair the burglary damage themselves.
- That the renter is bound to stay until the lease ends whatever the condition of the home.
- A wrong version of the Act.
