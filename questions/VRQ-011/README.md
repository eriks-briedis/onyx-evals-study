# VRQ-011

**Role:** date-sensitive  
**Set:** held-out (chosen, and its gold written, after the fix was frozen)

## Situation

A renter is charged a card-payment service fee through a rent app, while a slower manual option has no fee. The app is run by a payments company the agent uses, and the card fee is charged by that company.

## Question

My rent app charges a fee when I pay by card. The app is run by a payments company my agent uses, and it's that company that charges the card fee. There's also a manual bank transfer option with no fee, but it's slower. Can the app charge me the card fee? Does it matter whether the fee goes to the app company or to the agent?

**Boundary:** 31 Mar 2026: reg 21C, the fees banned under s 51A, takes effect. s 51A itself commenced 25 Nov 2025, and reg 21C is already printed in Regulations version 005 but says it has effect only from 31 March 2026. Between Act versions 111 and 115, s 51A was substituted (No. 46/2025): version 111 exempts the rental provider, the agent and banks; version 115 exempts only banks (ADIs).

**Question as asked:** It's the middle of January 2026 (Thursday the 15th). My rent app charges a fee when I pay by card. The app is run by a payments company my agent uses, and it's that company that charges the card fee. There's also a manual bank transfer option with no fee, but it's slower. Can the app charge me the card fee? Does it matter whether the fee goes to the app company or to the agent?

**Must cite (version in force on this date):**
- Residential Tenancies Act 1997, Authorised Version No. 111 (s 42(4), (7); s 51(3); s 51A)
- Residential Tenancies Regulations 2021, Authorised Version No. 005 (reg 21C, not yet in effect)

**Correct answer (short):** The landlord or agent must make sure a way to pay rent that costs you nothing extra (apart from your own bank's fees) is reasonably available, and must tell you about any third-party fees for a payment method before you agree to use it. A slower fee-free bank transfer is likely enough for that. The landlord or agent themselves can't charge you for setting up or using an electronic payment facility. A new ban on businesses charging fees around rent payments (s 51A) started on 25 November 2025, but on this date the list of banned fees (reg 21C) is not yet in effect: it applies from 31 March 2026. So right now the Act does not ban a card fee charged by the app company. Who collects the fee does matter: the landlord or agent are bound by the fee-free-method and no-charge-for-electronic-payment rules; the app company will be caught by the new ban once the list applies (unless the fee is a surcharge allowed under another law). What you can do: use the fee-free method, ask the agent in writing to confirm the fee-free option and the fee information you were given, and raise it with Consumer Affairs Victoria if no fee-free method is reasonably available.

**Must mention:**
- A payment method with no extra cost (apart from the renter's own bank fees) must be reasonably available, and the slower fee-free bank transfer may satisfy that.
- The ban on rent-payment fees is not yet in effect on this date: the list of banned fees (reg 21C) applies from 31 March 2026. (Saying the specific fee ban does not yet apply, without the date, also counts.)

**Optional (credit, not required):**
- Who collects the fee matters: the landlord or agent can't charge for setting up or using an electronic payment facility, while a third-party company is covered only by the new ban once it takes effect.

**Must not say:**
- That the card fee is already banned or illegal on this date under s 51A or reg 21C.
- That there is no rule requiring a fee-free way to pay rent.
- A wrong version of the Act or Regulations.

**Question as asked:** My rent app charges a fee when I pay by card. The app is run by a payments company my agent uses, and it's that company that charges the card fee. There's also a manual bank transfer option with no fee, but it's slower. Can the app charge me the card fee? Does it matter whether the fee goes to the app company or to the agent?

**Must cite (version in force on this date):**
- Residential Tenancies Act 1997, Authorised Version No. 115 (s 42(4), (7); s 51(3); s 51A)
- Residential Tenancies Regulations 2021, Authorised Version No. 009 (reg 21C)

**Correct answer (short):** The landlord or agent must make sure a way to pay rent that costs you nothing extra (apart from your own bank's fees) is reasonably available, and must tell you about any third-party fees before you agree to a payment method. On top of that, since 31 March 2026 anyone other than a bank is banned from demanding or receiving a fee for paying rent, unless it is a payment surcharge permitted under another law. That ban covers the app company and the agent or landlord alike, so it no longer matters who collects the fee; only a bank is exempt. Whether a card surcharge is "permitted under any other Act" is outside these documents. What you can do: pay by the fee-free method, tell the agent in writing that the fee appears to be banned under s 51A and reg 21C and ask for it to be stopped or refunded, and raise it with Consumer Affairs Victoria or apply to VCAT if it isn't.

**Must mention:**
- A payment method with no extra cost (apart from the renter's own bank fees) must be reasonably available.
- Fees for paying rent are banned for anyone other than a bank (s 51A with reg 21C), so it does not matter whether the app company or the agent collects it, unless the fee is a surcharge permitted under another law.
- At least one next step: use the fee-free method, ask for the fee to be stopped or refunded, or go to Consumer Affairs Victoria or VCAT.

**Must not say:**
- That the fee is allowed simply because a fee-free option exists.
- That the landlord or agent is exempt from the fee ban (that was the rule in version 111, not now).
- A wrong version of the Act or Regulations.

**Pass / fail rule:** Pass if the answer covers everything under "must mention", names the document and version it relies on (from the "must cite" list), and says nothing on the "must not say" list. Otherwise fail.
