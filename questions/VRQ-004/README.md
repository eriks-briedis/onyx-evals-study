# VRQ-004

**Role:** date-sensitive  
**Set:** main (one of the 10 questions the study was developed on)

## Situation

An agent emails a proposed rent increase. The renter negotiates, receives a rejection later, and says no prescribed notice form has arrived. The email arrives on the scenario date. It is an ordinary message with no Form 5 attached.

## Question

My agent emailed to say the rent is going up. I tried to negotiate and they said no, but I never got an official rent increase notice. Do I have to pay the higher rent, and from when?

**Boundary:** 25 Nov 2025: s 44 (rent increase notice) amended

## Gold — scenario date 2025-10-01

Status: approved 26 October 2026.

**Must cite (version in force on this date):**
- Residential Tenancies Act 1997, Authorised Version No. 110 (ss 44, 506(1); s 45 for the next step)
- Residential Tenancies Regulations 2021, Authorised Version No. 004 (reg 21, Form 5; Sch 1 Form 1 item 9)

**Correct answer (short):** A landlord must give at least 60 days notice of a rent increase, using the official form (Form 5). The notice must state the amount, how it was worked out, and the renter's right to ask the Director of Consumer Affairs Victoria to investigate within 30 days. Rent can't go up more than once in 12 months. A rent increase that doesn't follow these rules is invalid. An ordinary email without Form 5 is not a valid notice, so the renter doesn't have to pay the higher rent yet. Notices can be sent by email, but only under the Electronic Transactions (Victoria) Act 2000, which the standard lease links to the renter agreeing to email notices (item 9). If a proper Form 5 notice arrives later, the increase can start no sooner than 60 days after it is given. Until then the renter keeps paying the current rent. They can tell the agent in writing that no official notice has arrived, and if a proper notice comes and the increase seems too high, ask Consumer Affairs Victoria to investigate within 30 days.

**Must mention:**
- A rent increase needs notice on the official form (Form 5).
- At least 60 days notice.
- The email without the form is not a valid notice, so the higher rent isn't payable yet (an increase that breaks the rules is invalid).
- At least one next step: keep paying the current rent, tell the agent in writing, or ask Consumer Affairs Victoria to investigate once a proper notice arrives.

**Optional (credit, not required):**
- On a fixed-term lease, rent can't go up during the term unless the lease allows it.

**Must not say:**
- That the renter must pay the higher rent now.
- That the increase starts from the date of the email.
- A minimum notice period other than 60 days for a notice given on this date.
- That a notice can never be sent by email.
- A wrong version of the Act or Regulations.

**Pass / fail rule:** Pass if the answer covers everything under "must mention", names the document and version it relies on (from the "must cite" list), and says nothing on the "must not say" list. Otherwise fail.
