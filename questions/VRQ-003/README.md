# VRQ-003

**Role:** date-sensitive  
**Set:** main (one of the 10 questions the study was developed on)

## Situation

Renters receive a notice to vacate connected to a planned sale. The buyer withdraws, but the landlord does not withdraw the notice. The notice is given on the scenario date. The buyer pulls out a few days later, and the renters ask straight away.

## Question

Our landlord gave us a notice to vacate because they were selling. The buyer pulled out, but the landlord hasn't withdrawn the notice. Do we still have to leave?

**Boundary:** 25 Nov 2025: s 91ZZB (sale ground) amended

## Gold — scenario date 2025-10-01

Status: approved 26 October 2026.

**Must cite (version in force on this date):**
- Residential Tenancies Act 1997, Authorised Version No. 110 (ss 91ZZB, 91ZZO, 91ZZQ, 91ZZI(1), 91ZZS–91ZZT)

**Correct answer (short):** A landlord can give a notice to vacate because the home will be sold or offered for sale with vacant possession, or within 14 days of signing a sale contract (or of its conditions being met). The notice must give at least 60 days, be in the official form, state the reason, come with supporting evidence, and give the end date. On a fixed-term lease without a break clause, it has no effect if the end date is before the lease ends. The buyer pulling out does not cancel the notice by itself: a notice is withdrawn only by a written withdrawal signed by both the landlord and the renter. Whether the notice still holds depends on its stated ground and evidence: if the landlord still plans to sell with vacant possession it may stand, but if it relied on the failed contract the evidence may no longer support it. The renters can ask the landlord to withdraw it in writing, signed by both of them, or challenge it at VCAT within 30 days after it is given.

**Must mention:**
- The answer depends on the ground stated in the notice and its evidence (planned sale with vacant possession vs a signed contract).
- The minimum notice period of 60 days.
- The notice is withdrawn only in writing, signed by both the landlord and the renter.
- At least one next step: ask the landlord for a written withdrawal signed by both, or challenge the notice at VCAT within 30 days.

**Must not say:**
- That the buyer pulling out cancels the notice automatically.
- That the landlord can withdraw the notice alone.
- A minimum notice period other than 60 days for a notice given on this date.
- A wrong version of the Act.

**Pass / fail rule:** Pass if the answer covers everything under "must mention", names the document and version it relies on (from the "must cite" list), and says nothing on the "must not say" list. Otherwise fail.
