# VRQ-010

**Role:** partly outside the law documents  
**Set:** main (one of the 10 questions the study was developed on)

## Situation

A renter wants to arrange repairs to pet-damaged skirting boards without first informing the owner and also asks about insurance consequences. The renters are moving out soon (in the source story, to a smaller unit with the same landlord).

## Question

Our dog chewed the skirting boards. We're moving out soon. Can we just get someone to fix it quickly without telling the owner? Would it affect the owner's insurance on the property?

## Facts the question leaves open

- Insurance policy, insurer terms and repair specification are absent. Prohibited default insurance-excess term is not coverage evidence. Whether the proposed repair is a modification requires facts; s 64 is contextual, not a finding that all repairs require consent.

## Gold — scenario date 2026-10-15

**Must cite (version in force on this date):**
- Residential Tenancies Act 1997, Authorised Version No. 115 (ss 61, 62, 63; s 64 if raised)
- Residential Tenancies Regulations 2021, Authorised Version No. 009 (reg 11(h), insurance excess)

**Correct answer (short):** No. A renter who becomes aware of damage must tell the landlord in writing, as soon as practicable, that it happened and what it is (email is allowed). Renters must not intentionally or carelessly damage the home, and at the end of the lease must leave it in the same condition as when they moved in, allowing for fair wear and tear; pet damage isn't fair wear and tear. Without written consent, renters also can't make alterations beyond the small list of allowed ones, so it's safest to agree on the repair before doing it. Because they're moving out, the home's condition will be compared with when they moved in, so the damage needs to be dealt with either way. Rental law doesn't say whether this affects the owner's insurance; that depends on the policy, which isn't in these documents. A lease can't make the renter pay the owner's insurance excess by default. The renters should tell the landlord or agent in writing about the damage, offer to fix it or pay for it, and agree in writing on how and by whom the repair will be done. For insurance, the owner would need to check their policy or ask their insurer.

**Must mention:**
- The renter must tell the landlord in writing about the damage, as soon as practicable.
- The renter is responsible for damage they or their pet cause (not fair wear and tear), and must leave the home in the same condition.
- Rental law, or these documents, don't settle the insurance question.
- At least one next step: tell the landlord in writing, offer to fix or pay, or agree on the repair in writing.

**Must not say:**
- That a quiet repair without telling the landlord is fine.
- Any insurance outcome stated as fact (for example, "it will void the policy" or "it won't affect the insurance").
- That the renter must pay the owner's insurance excess by default (a lease term saying so is prohibited).
- A wrong version of the Act or Regulations.
